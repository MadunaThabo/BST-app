> The specification asks for a report that "highlights and provides insights into your journey through the design, development, presentation, demonstration and interactions with fellow team members". Insight, not narrative: the panel wants to see what you *learned about learning, about software, and about yourself*, with evidence. Suggested length 1 500–2 500 words. Use Gibbs' reflective cycle (Description → Feelings → Evaluation → Analysis → Conclusion → Action plan) in each section, or the simpler *What? So what? Now what?*

Keep a 3-line daily log from today (did / blocked / learned). Quote it. Specific beats general.

---

## 1. My role and expectations (≈ 150 words)
What I took on, why, what I expected it to involve, and what I was worried about on day one.

## 2. Design phase (≈ 300 words)
- How we chose BST + insertion; what I argued for and whether I was right.
- The ID model and learning theories: which one changed how I think about teaching a technical topic? Give one concrete feature that exists *because* of a theory.
- A design decision I disagreed with, how it was resolved, and what I think now.

## 3. Development phase (≈ 400 words)
- What I built or wrote; one problem that took longer than expected and why.
- Something I learned technically (e.g. trace-based animation, SVG layout, localStorage) or in content writing (writing a quiz item at the "Analyse" level is harder than it looks…).
- How the SE process (sprints, PRs, always-demo-able main) helped or got in the way. Evidence: a specific day/commit/PR.
- GenAI: how I used it, where it was wrong, how I verified.

## 4. Presentation and demonstration (≈ 300 words)
- Preparation and rehearsals; what changed between rehearsal 1 and the real thing.
- What went well and what did not on the day; a question from the panel or peers that I found hard and what I would answer now.
- Evaluating the other groups: what I noticed in their apps that I wish ours had — or was glad it didn't.

## 5. Working with the team (≈ 400 words)
- Communication: what worked (stand-ups? WhatsApp? pairing?) and what didn't.
- A moment of friction, how I handled it, how I'd handle it differently.
- What I contributed beyond my role; what I relied on others for.
- One thing I appreciated about a specific teammate (be concrete).

## 6. Insights about educational software (≈ 300 words)
- What I now believe about designing software *for learning* that I did not believe at the start of COS750.
- Connect to at least two lectures (e.g. motivation, assessment, learning styles caveat, special-needs design) — not as name-dropping but as "this changed what I built".

## 7. Action plan (≈ 150 words)
Three specific things I will do differently in my next group project / in my own teaching or development work.
